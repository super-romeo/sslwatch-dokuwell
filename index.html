How it works

An expired SSL certificate turns a working website into a full-page browser warning. Visitors leave, online shops stop taking orders, and connected apps or payment callbacks fail, often on a weekend when nobody is watching.

Automatic renewal has made this rarer, but not rare. Renewals fail when a DNS record changes, a server moves, a firewall blocks validation or a forgotten subdomain keeps an old certificate. The problem is usually discovered by a customer, not by the owner.

The service watches your certificates from the outside, the way a visitor's browser sees them. You add your domains and subdomains — the main site, the shop, the admin panel, the mail server, the API — and each one is checked every day.

For every host you see the certificate's expiry date, the issuer, the names it covers and whether the chain is complete. A clear list shows which certificates are fine, which expire soon and which have problems.

Alerts go out 30, 14 and 3 days before expiry by email or to a team chat. If a certificate that should renew automatically is still the old one close to its expiry, you get a separate alert that the renewal did not happen.

The service also warns when a certificate changes unexpectedly: a different issuer, a new key, or a certificate issued for your domain that you did not order. Such changes can mean a misconfiguration, or that someone else obtained a certificate for your name.

When a site goes down or the certificate is served incorrectly — wrong name, incomplete chain, an old certificate on one server behind a load balancer — the alert says what is wrong, so you know where to look.

Agencies and freelancers who look after many client sites can group hosts by client and send each client a short monthly report showing that their certificates are in order.

A history is kept for every host: when each certificate was issued and replaced, and every alert that was sent. When something breaks, you can see at a glance whether the certificate changed recently and which change came first.

Getting started takes a few minutes: add domains one by one or paste a list. Nothing needs to be installed on your servers; the checks run from outside.

Questions

Do I need to install anything on my server?

No, certificates are checked from outside, the way a browser sees them.

When do I get alerts?

30, 14 and 3 days before expiry, and whenever a renewal fails or a certificate changes unexpectedly.

Can I monitor subdomains and mail servers?

Yes, any host name that serves a certificate.

Does it renew certificates?

No. It monitors and alerts; renewal stays with you or your hosting provider.
